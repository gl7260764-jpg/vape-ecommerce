Yes, as a medicine. CBD (cannabidiol) is legal in Australia when a doctor or nurse practitioner prescribes it and a pharmacy dispenses it. The law also allows low-dose CBD as a pharmacist-only medicine you could buy without a prescription, but the TGA says no product has been registered for that yet. Hemp seed foods are legal; CBD oil sold as a supplement, or ordered from an overseas website, is not. Here's how the rules work as of November 2026, with links to the law itself, and how they differ by state.

<ShortAnswer>

- **Legal on prescription:** from a doctor or nurse practitioner, dispensed by a pharmacy.
- **No over-the-counter CBD yet:** the law allows a pharmacist-only CBD product of up to **150 mg a day**, but the TGA says none is registered.
- **Not from overseas websites,** even with a prescription. Driving with THC in your system is an offence in most states, prescription or not.

</ShortAnswer>

## How CBD is scheduled in Australia

Medicines and poisons are sorted into schedules by the national [Poisons Standard](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01327/asmade/2026-09-30/text/original/pdf) (the October 2026 edition is in force now). CBD has two entries:

| | Schedule 3: Pharmacist Only | Schedule 4: Prescription Only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you get it | From a pharmacist, no prescription | With a prescription, from a pharmacy |
| CBD content | 98% or more of all cannabinoids | 98% or more of all cannabinoids |
| THC | No more than 1% of cannabinoids | Other cannabinoids 2% or less |
| Dose | 150 mg a day or less | Not limited by the entry |
| Other conditions | Adults 18+, up to 30 days' supply, child-resistant pack, registered on the ARTG | |

A product with more than 2% of other cannabinoids, including THC, falls under the cannabis and THC entries instead: Schedule 8 when it's a medicine, and Schedule 9, prohibited, otherwise.

## Can you buy CBD oil over the counter?

Not yet. The pharmacist-only entry has existed since 2021, but only for products registered on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods. The [TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/explore-topic/medicinal-cannabis-hub/medicinal-cannabis-access-pathways-and-usage-data) says: "At present, however, there are no products on the ARTG that meet the requirements for this classification."

So a pharmacy can't sell you CBD without a prescription until a product like that is registered. CBD sold in a shop or online as a supplement isn't legal either. [HealthyWA](https://www.healthywa.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Cannabis) puts it plainly: "products labelled as containing CBD Oil cannot be sold as dietary supplements in Australia."

## How to get CBD legally: a prescription

"You can legally get medicinal cannabis products from a pharmacy with a prescription from a doctor or a nurse practitioner," says the [TGA's page for patients](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/explore-topic/medicinal-cannabis-hub/medicinal-cannabis-information-patients).

Only a couple of cannabis medicines are fully approved; the TGA lists Epidyolex (cannabidiol) and Sativex. Most CBD products are unapproved, and a prescriber reaches them through one of three pathways:

- **Special Access Scheme A:** a notification pathway for patients who are seriously ill.
- **Special Access Scheme B:** the prescriber applies to the TGA for approval for a patient in their care.
- **Authorised Prescriber:** a prescriber is approved to prescribe a category of product to a class of patients.

The TGA also notes that "each state and territory has different laws about medicinal cannabis", and some doctors need approval from their state or territory health department as well.

## Is hemp oil the same as CBD oil?

No. Hemp seed foods, including hemp seed oil, are legal food in Australia under Standard 1.4.4 of the [Food Standards Code](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00416/2017-11-12/2017-11-12/text/original/pdf), with strict limits:

| Hemp seed food | Total THC allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Hemp seeds | 5 mg/kg or less (hulled and non-viable for sale) |
| Hemp seed oil | 10 mg/kg or less |
| Hemp seed drinks | 0.2 mg/kg or less |
| Other hemp seed foods | 5 mg/kg or less |

The only cannabinoids allowed are those naturally on the seeds, CBD in any food can't exceed 75 mg/kg, and labels can't make a health claim about CBD. A bottle of hemp seed oil from the supermarket is a food, not a CBD medicine.

## Can I order CBD oil from overseas?

No. "You can't import medicinal cannabis products from overseas retailers or websites for personal use. Even with a prescription," says the [TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/explore-topic/medicinal-cannabis-hub/medicinal-cannabis-information-patients). Importing needs a licence and permit from the [Office of Drug Control](https://www.odc.gov.au/medicinal-cannabis/importing-medicinal-cannabis-products-australia).

The one exception is carrying it in yourself: "You must have a prescription to use a traveller's exemption. The product must match the prescription." It's a similar rule to the one for vapes; see [bringing vapes back from Bali](/blog/can-i-bring-vapes-back-from-bali-to-australia).

## CBD and driving, state by state

The CBD rules above are national. What changes between states is mostly driving, because roadside tests look for THC, and the TGA warns that "while some medicinal cannabis products don't list THC as an ingredient, they may contain traces of it". Nationally, the TGA says "you can be charged if THC is found in your body while driving. It doesn't matter if you have a prescription."

| State | What the rules say |
| --- | --- |
| NSW | "It's illegal to drive with THC in your system, including when it is used as a medicine." A law passed on 17 September 2026 will change this for registered patients, but isn't in effect yet ([Transport for NSW](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/roadsafety/topics-tips/drugs/medical-cannabis)). |
| Victoria | "Patients taking cannabidiol-only medicines can lawfully drive, as long as they are not impaired," but a prescription is no defence to a positive THC test ([Department of Transport](https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/road-rules-and-safety/alcohol-drugs-and-driving/medicinal-cannabis-and-driving)). |
| Queensland | "You can't use a valid prescription for medicinal cannabis to defend a charge if you test positive for THC" ([Queensland Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/licensing/healthy-to-drive/can-i-drive/medications-and-treatments/medicinal-cannabis)). |
| WA | Driving with THC in your system is an offence "regardless of whether the THC comes from prescribed legal medicinal cannabis". |
| Tasmania | No offence if the drug "was obtained and administered in accordance with the Poisons Act 1971" ([Road Safety Act, s 6A](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1970-077)), though driving while impaired still is. |

For the ACT, South Australia and the Northern Territory, check your transport department before you drive on any cannabis medicine.
