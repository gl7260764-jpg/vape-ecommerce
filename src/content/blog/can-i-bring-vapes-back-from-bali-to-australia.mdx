## How many vapes can I bring back from Bali?

Two vapes per person, plus up to 20 pods, cartridges or capsules and 200 ml of vape liquid. The limits are set by regulation 5A(2) of the [Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations 1956](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03651/latest/text), usually called the traveller exemption:

| What you're carrying | Limit per person |
| --- | --- |
| Vapes, disposable or refillable | 2 |
| Pods, cartridges or capsules | 20 |
| Vape liquid | 200 ml |

A few details that trip people up:

- **Disposables count.** The [Therapeutic Goods Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B00406/latest/text) include disposable vapes among the goods a traveller can bring in on a ship or aircraft, and each one is one of your two vapes.
- **The limits are per person.** Each traveller has their own allowance, which also covers someone in your care on the same flight.
- **It's for your own use.** The law describes the exemption as vapes you carry "for use in connection with the treatment" of yourself or someone in your care on the same flight. It isn't a way to bring vapes in for friends, or to sell.

The rules are the same at every Australian airport, whether you land in Perth, Darwin, Brisbane, Sydney or Melbourne, because the border is federal. What you can do with vapes once you're home is set by your state's law.

## Can I bring back flavours I bought in Bali?

The TGA's standard for vapes, [TGO 110](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00595/latest/text), sets rules such as which flavours and how much nicotine a vape can have. But section 6(1)(g) of the standard says it doesn't apply to vapes a passenger carries in under the traveller exemption. For vapes in your carry-on, the quantity limits above are what count.

## Do I need to declare vapes at the airport?

Declare them. Australian Border Force's guide to [what you can bring in](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/can-you-bring-it-in/categories) lists vaping goods under medicines and substances. Question 1 on the [Incoming Passenger Card](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-leaving-australia/files/ipc-sample-english.pdf) asks about "goods that may be prohibited or subject to restrictions, such as medicines", and the card says to tick Yes if you're unsure. Tick Yes, and tell the officer you're carrying vapes and how many.

Bringing more than the limit? Vapes over the allowance can be seized. Border Force has urged travellers "to use the surrender bins to dispose of any vapes prior to them being seized by our officers" ([ABF, March 2024](https://www.abf.gov.au/newsroom-subsite/Pages/18,000-vapes-seized-in-Sydney-as-crackdown-continues-26-03-2024.aspx)).

## Can I post vapes home from Bali?

No. Since 1 March 2024, importing vaping goods by post has been "prohibited absolutely", and vapes that arrive in the post are seized as prohibited imports ([ABF Customs Notice 2023/51](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2023-51.pdf)). That covers posting a box to yourself before you fly home and ordering from an overseas website. The same notice says "Australian patients will not be able to import vaping products from overseas regardless of whether they have a prescription".

## Flying with vapes: carry-on only

Airlines treat vapes as dangerous goods because of their lithium batteries. The rules from [Qantas](https://www.qantas.com/au/en/travel-info/baggage/dangerous-goods/personal-items-toiletries-and-medicinal-items.html) and [Virgin Australia](https://www.virginaustralia.com/au/en/travel-info/baggage/dangerous-goods/):

1. **Carry-on only.** Vapes are allowed in your cabin bag and not allowed in checked baggage.
2. **No vaping or charging on board.** In Qantas's words, "the use and charging of all e-cigarettes and other personal vaporisers are not permitted onboard an aircraft".
3. **Protect it from switching on.** Qantas says vapes "must be protected to prevent accidental activation", so keep yours in a case or a zipped pocket.
4. **Personal use only.** Virgin Australia says vapes "may only be carried for your personal use".

## Never carry THC or cannabis vapes

None of the above applies to cannabis. Indonesia's drug laws are among the toughest anywhere, and some drug offences carry the death penalty there. Don't buy, carry or use THC or CBD products in Bali, whatever a seller tells you, and read [Smartraveller's Indonesia advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/indonesia) before you go.
